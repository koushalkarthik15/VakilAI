# Document 10 — Legal Knowledge Base

**Project:** Indian Legal AI Assistant
**Version:** v0.1.0
**Status:** FROZEN
**Path:** `docs/10-legal-knowledge-base.md`

---

## 1. Purpose

The Legal Knowledge Base (Legal KB) is the structured, source-backed repository of legal rules, legal sources, applicability conditions, clause patterns, and verified government routes used by the Indian Legal AI Assistant.

The Legal KB exists to ensure that substantive legal findings are grounded in verified legal material rather than generated from model memory.

The Legal KB is a **knowledge and provenance layer**, not a replacement for legal research or professional legal advice.

The system must use the Legal KB to:

* identify relevant legal rules;
* determine whether a rule applies to the current document and jurisdiction;
* provide verified sources for substantive legal claims;
* connect findings to stable rule and source identifiers;
* support clause-level evidence;
* identify verified government procedures or routes where applicable;
* prevent unsupported legal claims from reaching the user.

---

# 2. Scope

The v1 Legal KB is limited to the project's frozen scope.

### 2.1 Document types

The Legal KB initially supports:

1. Rental / Lease Agreements
2. Freelancer / Service Agreements

Rules unrelated to these document types should not be treated as active v1 knowledge.

### 2.2 Jurisdiction

The initial jurisdiction scope is:

* Telangana state law and applicable Telangana rules/regulations;
* applicable central Indian legislation and regulations relevant to the supported document types and intended Telangana/Hyderabad use case.

The Legal KB is **not** a comprehensive nationwide Indian legal database.

### 2.3 Languages

Legal rules are stored in a canonical structured representation.

The system may present explanations in:

* English
* Telugu
* Hindi

Changing the presentation language must not change the underlying legal rule or reasoning.

---

# 3. Core Principles

## 3.1 Source before claim

Every substantive legal claim exposed to the user must have a verified supporting source.

The system must never create a legal claim merely because an AI model considers it plausible.

---

## 3.2 Rule existence is not rule applicability

The presence of a rule in the Legal KB does not mean that the rule applies to every document.

Applicability must consider relevant conditions such as:

* jurisdiction;
* document type;
* subject matter;
* parties or party roles where relevant;
* effective date;
* transaction/context;
* statutory conditions;
* exclusions or exceptions;
* available document evidence.

---

## 3.3 Missing information is not negative information

If the document does not contain sufficient evidence to determine whether something exists, the system must not infer its absence.

Use:

`NOT_STATED`

when a contract term cannot be established from the available document.

Example:

> The agreement does not state a notice period.

This is different from:

> The agreement has no notice period.

The second statement requires stronger evidence.

---

## 3.4 Legal facts, document facts, and interpretation remain separate

The Legal KB represents legal knowledge.

It must not be confused with:

* facts extracted from the user's document;
* conclusions generated by an AI model;
* user-provided facts;
* legal advice.

The architecture therefore maintains three distinct categories:

### Document fact

Something directly supported by the uploaded document.

Example:

`Clause C-014 states that rent is payable on the 5th of each month.`

### Legal rule

A source-backed rule represented in the Legal KB.

Example:

`RULE-TG-LEASE-001`

### AI interpretation

A structured interpretation connecting the document fact with applicable legal knowledge.

Example:

`Clause C-014 may require comparison against RULE-TG-LEASE-001.`

These categories must remain distinguishable in application data and UI.

# 4. Legal Source Hierarchy

The Legal KB uses the following source hierarchy.

## Tier 1 — Official legislation and government authority

Preferred sources include:

* Acts;
* statutes;
* rules;
* regulations;
* official legislative publications;
* official gazette material;
* other authoritative government-issued legal instruments.

These sources form the primary basis for substantive legal rules wherever available.

---

## Tier 2 — Official courts and tribunals

Examples include:

* Supreme Court;
* High Courts;
* tribunals;
* official judgments/orders;
* official court publications.

These sources may be used where judicial interpretation or procedural information is relevant.

---

## Tier 3 — Official government departments and portals

Examples include:

* Telangana government departments;
* central government departments;
* official registration portals;
* official procedural portals;
* official notifications and government guidance.

These sources are particularly relevant to government routes and procedures.

---

## Tier 4 — Established secondary legal sources

Examples may include:

* established legal publishers;
* recognized legal databases;
* professional legal commentary.

These may provide useful context or interpretation but must not automatically be treated as equivalent to primary authority.

---

## Tier 5 — General secondary sources

Examples include:

* general educational material;
* general legal explainers;
* non-authoritative informational resources.

These may assist understanding but should not independently establish a substantive legal claim when a stronger authoritative source is required.

# 5. Legal Knowledge Base Entities

The initial Legal KB consists of the following conceptual entities:

1. `LegalDomain`
2. `LegalRule`
3. `LegalSource`
4. `ClausePattern`
5. `GovernmentRoute`

Additional fields may represent applicability periods and conditions without requiring separate entities.

---

# 6. LegalDomain

A `LegalDomain` groups related legal knowledge.

Example domains include:

* lease and rental;
* security deposits;
* rent and payment;
* termination;
* notice;
* maintenance and repairs;
* freelancer/service obligations;
* payment terms;
* termination of services;
* intellectual property where relevant;
* dispute resolution;
* registration/procedural requirements.

The exact domain list is extensible.

### Suggested structure

```text
LegalDomain
├── domain_id
├── name
├── description
└── status
```

### Rules

* `domain_id` must be stable.
* Domains must describe legal subject areas rather than individual user cases.
* A domain must not contain user-specific PII.

---

# 7. LegalSource

A `LegalSource` represents a verified source supporting legal knowledge.

### Suggested structure

```text
LegalSource
├── source_id
├── title
├── authority
├── source_type
├── tier
├── jurisdiction
├── url
├── citation
├── verification_status
├── verified_at
├── retrieved_at
├── effective_from
├── effective_to
└── notes
```

### Required properties

Every source must have:

* stable `source_id`;
* identifiable title;
* authority;
* source type;
* source tier;
* jurisdiction;
* source URL or authoritative citation where applicable;
* verification status.

### Verification status

Suggested statuses:

```text
VERIFIED
PENDING_VERIFICATION
OUTDATED
REQUIRES_REVIEW
RETIRED
```

Only sources appropriate for the current knowledge state may support active legal claims.

---

# 8. Source URLs

Source URLs are treated as controlled data.

The AI must never invent legal URLs.

A URL shown to the user must originate from a verified Legal KB record or another explicitly verified source mechanism.

The system must not construct a URL by guessing:

* government domains;
* statute paths;
* section URLs;
* court URLs;
* registration portal URLs.

If a verified URL is unavailable, the system may provide the source citation without inventing a link.

---

# 9. LegalRule

A `LegalRule` represents a structured legal proposition that can potentially apply to a supported document.

### Suggested structure

```text
LegalRule
├── rule_id
├── domain_id
├── title
├── description
├── jurisdiction
├── document_types
├── applicability_conditions
├── exclusions
├── effective_from
├── effective_to
├── source_ids
├── status
├── last_verified_at
└── notes
```

---

# 10. Rule Requirements

Every active `LegalRule` must:

1. have a stable `rule_id`;
2. identify its legal domain;
3. identify its jurisdiction;
4. specify supported document type applicability;
5. describe relevant applicability conditions;
6. identify its effective period where relevant;
7. reference at least one supporting `LegalSource`;
8. have a verification status;
9. have sufficient provenance for human review.

A rule without a valid supporting source must not be active for substantive legal analysis.

---

# 11. Rule Applicability

Rule retrieval must be deterministic and explicit wherever possible.

A rule may be considered applicable only after evaluating relevant dimensions.

Conceptually:

```text
Rule Applicability =
    Jurisdiction
    AND Document Type
    AND Subject Matter
    AND Effective Date
    AND Applicable Conditions
    AND No Applicable Exclusion
```

Not every rule requires every dimension.

The implementation must evaluate only conditions relevant to that rule.

---

# 12. Jurisdiction States

Jurisdiction determination must distinguish:

```text
SUPPORTED
OUTSIDE_SCOPE
JURISDICTION_UNCLEAR
```

### SUPPORTED

The available information places the case within the supported Telangana/central-law scope.

### OUTSIDE_SCOPE

The document or situation is outside the supported jurisdiction scope.

### JURISDICTION_UNCLEAR

The available evidence is insufficient to determine the relevant jurisdiction.

The system must not silently treat `JURISDICTION_UNCLEAR` as `SUPPORTED`.

---

# 13. Effective Dates

Legal rules may change over time.

The Legal KB must therefore support:

* `effective_from`;
* `effective_to`;
* verification timestamps;
* source version or citation where applicable.

When analyzing a document, the system should use the legal rule applicable to the relevant date when that date can be established.

If the relevant date cannot be established and the difference could materially affect the finding, the system must surface the uncertainty rather than silently selecting a rule.

---

# 14. Rule-to-Source Relationship

Every active legal rule must maintain an explicit relationship to its supporting source.

Example:

```text
RULE-TG-LEASE-001
    ↓
SRC-TG-2026-001
```

A rule may reference multiple sources when appropriate.

The relationship must preserve provenance.

The system should be able to answer:

> "Why is this legal rule present?"

with a chain such as:

```text
Finding
  ↓
Legal Rule
  ↓
Legal Source
  ↓
Authority / Citation
```

---

# 15. ClausePattern

A `ClausePattern` represents a recognizable contractual pattern that may help identify relevant document language.

It is **not itself a legal conclusion**.

### Suggested structure

```text
ClausePattern
├── pattern_id
├── domain_id
├── document_types
├── description
├── detection_signals
├── related_rule_ids
└── status
```

Examples of patterns:

* rent/payment clause;
* security deposit clause;
* termination clause;
* notice clause;
* maintenance clause;
* service payment clause;
* intellectual-property clause;
* dispute-resolution clause.

A clause pattern may identify candidate clauses for further analysis.

It must not state that a detected clause is automatically:

* legal;
* illegal;
* enforceable;
* unenforceable;
* compliant;
* non-compliant.

Those conclusions require applicable legal rules and evidence.

---

# 16. GovernmentRoute

A `GovernmentRoute` represents a verified official government or institutional procedure relevant to a user action.

### Suggested structure

```text
GovernmentRoute
├── route_id
├── name
├── purpose
├── authority
├── jurisdiction
├── applicable_document_types
├── conditions
├── official_url
├── required_information
├── status
├── verified_at
└── notes
```

Examples may include verified routes for:

* registration-related procedures;
* official complaint mechanisms;
* government department processes;
* other relevant procedural actions.

Government routes must use verified official destinations.

The system must not fabricate a government procedure merely because it appears plausible.

---

# 17. Legal Claim Protocol

All substantive legal findings must follow this structure conceptually:

```text
Document Evidence
        ↓
Applicable Legal Rule
        ↓
Verified Legal Source
        ↓
Structured Interpretation
        ↓
User-Facing Explanation
```

A finding should therefore be traceable to:

* one or more clause IDs;
* one or more rule IDs;
* one or more source IDs.

---

# 18. Insufficient Source

If the system cannot establish a substantive legal claim from an adequate source, it must use:

```text
INSUFFICIENT_SOURCE
```

This state must be visible to the user when relevant.

It must not be silently converted into a confident legal statement.

Example:

> The available knowledge base does not contain a sufficiently verified source to support a conclusion about this clause.

The system may still explain the document language itself if that explanation is clearly identified as document-derived rather than legal authority.

---

# 19. Missing Contract Information

The Legal KB does not determine missing contractual facts.

When document extraction cannot establish a term, use:

```text
NOT_STATED
```

Example:

```text
Clause:
  notice_period

Document status:
  NOT_STATED
```

The system must not infer:

```text
notice_period = 0
```

or:

```text
notice_period = legally invalid
```

without supporting evidence.

---

# 20. Source Verification Workflow

Legal KB content should enter the active knowledge base through a controlled workflow.

### Step 1 — Identify source

Record:

* source title;
* authority;
* source type;
* jurisdiction;
* URL/citation;
* publication information where available.

### Step 2 — Verify authority

Determine the source tier and whether it is authoritative for the intended claim.

### Step 3 — Extract rule

Represent the relevant legal proposition in structured form.

### Step 4 — Record applicability

Document:

* jurisdiction;
* document type;
* conditions;
* exclusions;
* effective period.

### Step 5 — Link provenance

Attach the rule to one or more verified source IDs.

### Step 6 — Review

Check:

* wording;
* scope;
* effective date;
* source authority;
* applicability conditions;
* contradictions with existing knowledge.

### Step 7 — Activate

Only verified and sufficiently specified rules become active.

---

# 21. Knowledge Base Updates

Legal knowledge is not static.

When a law, rule, notification, procedure, or authoritative interpretation changes:

1. identify the affected source;
2. create or update the appropriate source record;
3. determine affected rules;
4. update effective periods;
5. preserve historical provenance;
6. mark outdated rules appropriately;
7. review dependent clause patterns and government routes;
8. run relevant regression tests;
9. record the change in the Decision Log when it changes a project-level decision.

Historical records should not be silently overwritten when doing so would destroy provenance.

---

# 22. Conflict and Ambiguity Handling

The Legal KB must not silently resolve substantive legal conflicts.

If credible sources create an unresolved conflict:

```text
LEGAL_KB_AMBIGUITY
```

should be represented internally or surfaced through an equivalent structured status.

The system must:

* preserve the relevant sources;
* identify the conflict;
* avoid presenting one interpretation as certain without sufficient basis;
* escalate for human review when necessary.

The engineering rule is:

> Stop on material ambiguity rather than fabricate certainty.

---

# 23. Secondary Sources

Tier 4 and Tier 5 sources may be useful for:

* explanations;
* contextual understanding;
* terminology;
* discovery of potentially relevant primary sources.

They are not automatically equivalent to:

* statutes;
* regulations;
* official government material;
* court decisions.

When a substantive claim can be supported by authoritative primary material, that material should be preferred.

---

# 24. Legal KB Retrieval

The application should retrieve applicable knowledge using deterministic filtering before involving an AI model.

Conceptually:

```text
Document Classification
        ↓
Jurisdiction
        ↓
Relevant Legal Domains
        ↓
Applicable Rules
        ↓
Relevant Sources
        ↓
Compact AI Context
```

Only rules that are potentially applicable should be sent to the AI reasoning layer.

The AI should not receive the entire Legal KB for every request.

This improves:

* accuracy;
* explainability;
* token efficiency;
* latency;
* quota usage;
* resistance to irrelevant knowledge.

---

# 25. AI Interaction

The Legal KB is an authoritative application data source from the application's perspective.

AI models receive structured legal context such as:

```text
rule_id
rule_summary
applicability
source_id
source_citation
source_tier
effective_period
```

The AI may:

* interpret applicable rules;
* explain their relationship to document clauses;
* identify questions or uncertainty;
* produce structured findings.

The AI may not:

* invent rules;
* invent statutes;
* invent sections;
* invent case law;
* invent government procedures;
* invent source IDs;
* invent URLs;
* modify the Legal KB;
* override applicability conditions.

---

# 26. AI Output Validation

AI-generated findings must be validated before becoming application state.

Validation must check:

* schema validity;
* referenced clause IDs;
* referenced rule IDs;
* referenced source IDs;
* supported status values;
* severity values;
* confidence/status separation;
* required evidence fields.

Unknown IDs must cause validation failure or controlled rejection.

The application must never trust an AI-generated identifier merely because it is syntactically valid.

---

# 27. Legal Knowledge and Severity

Severity describes the **potential consequence or significance of an identified issue to the user**.

It does not represent:

* confidence;
* probability of winning a dispute;
* probability of enforcement;
* legal certainty;
* litigation outcome.

The Legal KB must not encode severity as an intrinsic property of every rule.

The same rule may have different practical significance depending on the document context and affected user.

Therefore:

```text
Legal Rule ≠ Severity
```

Severity belongs to the analysis/finding layer.

---

# 28. Data Quality Requirements

Every active Legal KB record must satisfy relevant validation rules.

### Sources

* valid stable ID;
* identifiable authority;
* valid source tier;
* jurisdiction recorded;
* verification state recorded;
* provenance available.

### Rules

* stable ID;
* legal domain;
* jurisdiction;
* applicability conditions;
* effective period where relevant;
* source relationship;
* active status.

### Clause patterns

* stable ID;
* supported document type;
* domain;
* detection definition;
* related rules where applicable.

### Government routes

* stable ID;
* official authority;
* jurisdiction;
* verified official destination;
* applicability conditions.

---

# 29. No User PII in the Legal KB

The Legal KB is shared application knowledge.

It must never contain case-specific personal information such as:

* names;
* phone numbers;
* email addresses;
* Aadhaar numbers;
* PAN numbers;
* bank information;
* addresses;
* user-specific contract contents.

User document data belongs to the active case/session context, not the static Legal KB.

---

# 30. Testing Requirements

The Legal KB must have automated tests for:

### Source integrity

* missing source IDs;
* invalid source relationships;
* unverified sources;
* invalid source tiers.

### Rule applicability

* supported jurisdiction;
* outside jurisdiction;
* unclear jurisdiction;
* supported document type;
* unsupported document type;
* effective-date boundaries;
* exclusions.

### Provenance

* every active rule has a valid source;
* every finding can resolve its referenced rule/source.

### Missing information

* `NOT_STATED` is preserved;
* missing document terms are not inferred.

### Insufficient evidence

* unsupported claims produce `INSUFFICIENT_SOURCE`;
* fabricated sources are rejected.

### Regression

Changes to Legal KB records must trigger relevant analysis tests.

---

# 31. Versioning and Provenance

Legal knowledge changes must remain auditable.

Where practical, records should preserve:

* creation timestamp;
* modification timestamp;
* verification timestamp;
* source version/citation;
* effective dates;
* status transitions;
* reason for change.

If a rule is superseded, the historical record should remain identifiable.

---

# 32. Search and Indexing

The initial Legal KB does not require a vector database.

For v1, deterministic retrieval should be preferred where sufficient:

* document type;
* jurisdiction;
* legal domain;
* rule status;
* effective date;
* applicability conditions;
* explicit identifiers.

Semantic search may be introduced later if testing demonstrates that deterministic retrieval is insufficient.

Any future retrieval system must preserve the same source and provenance requirements.

---

# 33. Security Requirements

Legal KB administration must be treated as a privileged operation.

The application must prevent untrusted user input or AI output from directly modifying legal rules or sources.

AI-generated content must never become active Legal KB data without a controlled validation and review process.

---

# 34. Relationship to Document Analysis

The Legal KB does not replace document extraction.

The analysis pipeline remains:

```text
PDF
 ↓
Text / Structure Extraction
 ↓
Document Classification
 ↓
Clause Identification
 ↓
Legal KB Applicability
 ↓
AI Reasoning Where Needed
 ↓
Structured Finding
 ↓
Evidence + Source
```

The Legal KB supplies legal knowledge; the uploaded document supplies case-specific evidence.

---

# 35. Relationship to Five-Stage Workflow

## Stage 1 — Understand

Legal KB usage is limited.

The primary goal is to understand the document and identify its structure and type.

---

## Stage 2 — Flag

Applicable legal rules are retrieved to identify potentially consequential issues.

Each substantive finding should connect:

```text
Clause → Rule → Source
```

---

## Stage 3 — Compare

The Legal KB provides the legal baseline against which contractual language may be compared.

The system must distinguish:

* what the contract says;
* what the applicable legal rule says;
* what the comparison means.

---

## Stage 4 — Act

Applicable verified routes may be retrieved from:

* `GovernmentRoute`;
* verified legal sources;
* structured action guidance.

No autonomous legal action is performed.

---

## Stage 5 — Prepare

The Legal KB may support preparation material such as:

* questions to ask;
* information to gather;
* documents to prepare;
* verified procedural routes.

Preparation must remain distinct from legal advice.

---

# 36. Failure Modes

The system must fail safely when:

* a source cannot be verified;
* a rule has no valid source;
* jurisdiction is unclear;
* effective dates cannot be resolved where material;
* rule applicability is ambiguous;
* AI references unknown IDs;
* source URLs are unavailable;
* the Legal KB is unavailable.

Safe failure may result in:

```text
INSUFFICIENT_SOURCE
JURISDICTION_UNCLEAR
LEGAL_KB_AMBIGUITY
NOT_STATED
```

The system must not replace these states with fabricated certainty.

---

# 37. Observability

The system should log non-sensitive Legal KB identifiers needed to understand analysis behavior, including:

* rule IDs selected;
* source IDs selected;
* applicability decision;
* knowledge version where available;
* validation failures;
* insufficient-source events.

Logs must not unnecessarily contain user PII or full document text.

# 39. Final Principle

The Legal Knowledge Base exists to make the system's legal reasoning **traceable, bounded, and source-backed**.

The fundamental relationship is:

```text
Verified Source
      ↓
Legal Rule
      ↓
Applicability
      ↓
Document Evidence
      ↓
Structured Interpretation
      ↓
User Explanation
```

The system must prefer an explicit **"insufficient source"** or **"jurisdiction unclear"** state over an unsupported legal conclusion.

**No fabricated law. No fabricated sources. No fabricated certainty.**


## MongoDB Persistence
The Legal Knowledge Base will use MongoDB as its persistent runtime store. It must store application knowledge (LegalDomain, LegalRule, LegalSource) and MUST NOT contain user PDFs, PII, or private analysis. The KB must remain reproducible and source-backed via version-controlled definitions seeded into MongoDB.


## Effective-Date Filtering
Legal rules are deterministically filtered before AI reasoning. Flow: Legal KB -> Retrieve candidates -> Jurisdiction -> Document-type -> Effective-date (`effective_from`, `effective_to`) -> Applicability -> AnalysisContext. The system must not knowingly provide repealed or future rules to the AI.
